S/O/D Rating Tables

Severity (S) — PFMEA (Impact to Plant / Ship-to-Plant / End User)

10
High: Failure may result in an acute health/safety risk for the manufacturing or assembly worker. For the end user: affects safe operation of the vehicle, or the health of the driver, passenger, or road user/pedestrian.
9
High: Failure may result in in-plant regulatory noncompliance. For the end user: noncompliance with regulations.
8
Moderately High: 100% of production run affected may have to be scrapped; may result in in-plant regulatory noncompliance or chronic health/safety risk for the worker. Ship-to-plant: shutdown greater than a full production shift, stop shipment possible, field repair/replacement required. End user: loss of primary vehicle function necessary for normal driving.
7
Moderately High: Product may be sorted and a portion (<100%) scrapped; deviation from primary process, decreased line speed or added manpower. Ship-to-plant: shutdown from 1 hour up to a full shift. End user: degradation of primary vehicle function necessary for normal driving.
6
Moderately Low: 100% of production run may have to be reworked off-line and accepted. Ship-to-plant: shutdown up to 1 hour. End user: loss of secondary vehicle function.
5
Moderately Low: A portion of the production run may have to be reworked off-line and accepted; no line shutdown but sort required. End user: degradation of secondary vehicle function.
4
Moderately Low: 100% of production run may have to be reworked in-station before processing; triggers a significant reaction plan. End user: very objectionable appearance, sound, vibration, harshness, or haptics.
3
Low: A portion of the production run may have to be reworked in-station before processing; triggers a minor reaction plan. End user: moderately objectionable appearance, sound, vibration, harshness, or haptics.
2
Low: Slight inconvenience to process, operation, or operator; no reaction plan triggered, feedback to supplier required. End user: slightly objectionable appearance, sound, vibration, harshness, or haptics.
1
Very Low: No discernible effect.

Severity (S) — DFMEA (Vehicle Function Only)

10
Very High: Affects safe operation of the vehicle and/or other vehicles, the health of driver or passenger(s), or road users/pedestrians.
9
Noncompliance with regulations.
8
High: Loss of primary vehicle function necessary for normal driving during expected service life.
7
Degradation of primary vehicle function necessary for normal driving during expected service life.
6
Moderate: Loss of secondary vehicle function.
5
Degradation of secondary vehicle function.
4
Very objectionable appearance, sound, vibration, harshness, or haptics.
3
Low: Moderately objectionable appearance, sound, vibration, harshness, or haptics.
2
Slightly objectionable appearance, sound, vibration, harshness, or haptics.
1
Very Low: No discernible effect.

Occurrence (O) — PFMEA (Prevention Control Effectiveness)

10
Extremely High (No prevention controls): No prevention controls in place.
9-8
Very High (Behavioral): Prevention controls will have little effect in preventing the failure cause.
7-6
High (Behavioral or Technical): Prevention controls are somewhat effective in preventing the failure cause.
5-4
Moderate: Prevention controls are effective in preventing the failure cause.
3-2
Low / Very Low (Best Practices): Prevention controls are highly effective in preventing the failure cause.
1
Extremely Low (Technical): Prevention controls are extremely effective in preventing the failure cause from occurring, due to design (e.g. part geometry) or process (e.g. fixture/tooling design). Failure Mode cannot be physically produced.

Occurrence (O) — DFMEA (Design/Technology Maturity & Field Experience)

10
Extremely High: First application of new technology anywhere without operating experience and/or under uncontrolled operating conditions. No verification/validation experience. Standards and best practices do not yet exist. Prevention controls not able to predict field performance or do not exist.
9
Very High: First use of design with technical innovations or materials within the company. New application or change in duty cycle/operating conditions. No verification/validation experience. Prevention controls not targeted to identify performance to specific requirements.
8
Very High: First use of design with technical innovations or materials on a new application. No verification/validation experience. Few existing standards/best practices apply. Prevention controls not a reliable indicator of field performance.
7
High: New design based on similar technology and materials. New application or change in duty cycle/operating conditions. Standards/best practices apply to the baseline design but not the innovations. Prevention controls provide limited indication of performance.
6
High: Similar to previous designs using existing technology/materials. Similar application with changes in duty cycle/operating conditions. Previous testing or field experience. Standards/design rules exist but are insufficient to ensure the failure cause won't occur. Prevention controls provide some ability to prevent a failure cause.
5
Moderate: Detail changes to a previous design using proven technology/materials. Similar application/duty cycle. Previous testing/field experience, or new design with some related test experience. Design addresses lessons learned. Best practices re-evaluated but not yet proven. Prevention controls capable of finding deficiencies and provide some indication of performance.
4
Moderate: Almost identical design with short-term field exposure. Similar application, minor change in duty cycle/operating conditions. Previous testing or field experience. Predecessor design conforms to best practices, standards, and specifications. Prevention controls capable of finding deficiencies and indicate likely design conformance.
3
Low: Detail changes to a known design (same application, minor duty-cycle change) and testing/field experience under comparable conditions, or new design with a successfully completed test procedure. Design expected to conform to standards/best practices considering lessons learned. Prevention controls predict conformance of the production design.
2
Very Low: Almost identical mature design with long field exposure. Same application, comparable duty cycle/operating conditions. Testing/field experience under comparable conditions. Design expected to conform to standards/best practices with significant confidence margin. Prevention controls indicate confidence in design conformance.
1
Extremely Low: Failure eliminated through prevention control; failure cause is not possible by design.

Detection (D) — DFMEA (Product Design Validation)

10
Very Low: Test procedure yet to be developed; test method not defined.
9
Very Low: Test method not designed specifically to detect the failure mode or cause (Pass-Fail, Test-to-Failure, Degradation Testing).
8
Low: New test method, not yet proven (Pass-Fail, Test-to-Failure, Degradation Testing).
7
Low: Proven test method for verification/validation, but timing is later in the development cycle — test failures may cause production delays for re-design/re-tooling (Pass-Fail Testing).
6-5
Moderate: Same maturity as D=7 (timing later in development cycle) — verified via Test-to-Failure (6) or Degradation Testing (5).
4-2
High: Proven test method with timing sufficient to modify production tools before release — Pass-Fail Testing (4), Test-to-Failure (3), or Degradation Testing (2).
1
Very High: Prior testing confirmed the failure mode/cause cannot occur, or detection methods proven to always detect it.

Detection (D) — PFMEA (Process Design Validation)

10
Very Low: No testing or inspection method established or known; the failure mode will not or cannot be detected.
9
Very Low: Unlikely the testing/inspection method will detect the failure mode; not easily caught by random or sporadic audits.
8-7
Low: Test/inspection method not proven effective/reliable — human inspection (8) or machine-based inspection equipment (7) that "should" detect the failure.
6-5
Moderate: Test/inspection method proven effective/reliable — human inspection (6) or machine-based inspection (5), including product sample checks, that "will" detect the failure.
4-3
High: Automated detection system proven effective/reliable — detects downstream (4) or in-station (3), preventing outflow via a robust reject-control system.
2
High: Machine-based detection proven effective/reliable that detects the cause and prevents the failure mode (discrepant part) from being produced.
1
Very High: Failure mode cannot be physically produced as-designed/processed, or detection methods proven to always detect it.
