1. System Overview & Access Roles

Architecture, dual user personas, authentication, and workspace navigation
The Adhesive FMEA Quality Management System is designed to create, analyze, review, and govern Failure Mode and Effects Analysis documents tailored specifically for adhesive manufacturing. It enforces strict separation of responsibilities:
Operator (Preparer / Editor) Drafts formulations, fills DFMEA & PFMEA worksheets, configures Control Plans, imports spreadsheets, and submits documents for formal review.
HOD (Head of Department) Inspects high-risk checkpoints (RPN > 100), signs off approvals or rejections with audit remarks, and locks production releases. Default HOD passkey is 123.
Key Features & Capabilities 5 Features
  • Role-based access control with distinct Operator and HOD permissions.
  • Instant user registration and persistent local session management.
  • Top active formulation switcher that dynamically binds all open worksheets.
  • Dark mode and Light mode high-contrast display switching.
  • Collapsible sidebar navigation for seamless access to all quality modules.
Enterprise Quality Governance
Zero-Defect Adhesive Engineering

2. Dashboard Overview

Executive command center, operational metrics, and risk distribution
The Dashboard provides real-time visibility into your entire adhesive FMEA portfolio. It aggregates project counts, tracks risk scoring thresholds, visualizes RPN distribution bands, and presents a quick audit log of recent operator activities.
Key Features & Capabilities 5 Features
  • 4 Live KPI metric cards: Active Formulations, Critical Risks, High Risks, and Released Quality Reports.
  • Interactive RPN Distribution chart visualizing risk spread across Low, Medium, High, and Critical thresholds.
  • Adhesive Product Family distribution summary (Hot Melt, Water-based, Solvent, Structural).
  • Live Activity & Operation log snapshot showing recent user modifications.
  • Quick-action navigation buttons to jump directly to active projects or review queues.
Operational Intelligence
Instant Risk Prioritization

3. Master Data

Standardized engineering baseline across 4 core master pillars
Master Data forms the technical backbone of the Adhesive FMEA system. It categorizes baseline engineering parameters across 4 foundational pillars:
  1. Product Master: Target adhesive specifications, bonding strength, curing time, and thermal limits.
  2. Raw Material Master: Polymers, resins, tackifiers, plasticizers, crosslinkers, and fillers.
  3. Application Master: Dispensing methods, substrate types (metal, plastics, composites), and environmental conditions.
  4. Packaging Master: Cartridges, drums, pails, nozzle barriers, moisture scavengers, and shelf-life ratings.
Key Features & Capabilities 5 Features
  • 4 Master Database Pillars with dedicated tabs and standardized data columns.
  • Pre-populated adhesive industry parameters ready for immediate deployment.
  • Full CRUD operations: Add custom items, edit parameters, and delete obsolete records.
  • Instant column search and category filtering for fast reference lookups.
  • Direct integration feeding master records into DFMEA auto-suggest pickers.
Standardized Master Data
Consistent Engineering Specifications

4. Formulations Management

Project creation, version revision control, and active context binding
The Formulations module manages product projects and lifecycle progression. Each adhesive formula (e.g. AG111, EPO-700) maintains its own linked DFMEA worksheets, PFMEA worksheets, and Control Plans under strict revision tracking.
Key Features & Capabilities 5 Features
  • Create new adhesive formulations with custom name, product code, family, and description.
  • Global active formulation selector in the header binding all DFMEA, PFMEA, and Control Plan data.
  • Revision code tracking (Rev A, Rev B, Rev C) for continuous engineering change management.
  • Document lifecycle states: Draft, Pending Review, and Approved.
  • Clone formulation utility to quickly duplicate and adapt existing formulas for new customer variants.
Precision Chemistry
Structured Lifecycle Management

5. Design FMEA (DFMEA)

Comprehensive product design risk analysis across the 4 Master Data Pillars
DFMEA systematically identifies potential design failure modes, evaluated through Severity (S), Occurrence (O), and Detection (D) ratings to calculate the Risk Priority Number:
Formula: RPN = Severity (S) × Occurrence (O) × Detection (D)
Key Features & Capabilities 6 Features
  • 4 Sub-Tabs for all 4 Pillars: 1. Product Master, 2. Raw Material Master, 3. Application Master, 4. Packaging Master.
  • Standard columns: Process Step, Function, Failure Mode, Effect, Severity (S), Cause, Occurrence (O), Prevention, Detection, Detection (D).
  • Automatic RPN calculation with real-time risk severity color tags (Critical ≥ 200, High ≥ 100, Medium, Low).
  • Corrective action assignment: Recommended Actions, Responsible Person, Target Date, Actions Taken, and Revised S/O/D ratings.
  • One-click historical failure mode injection from the History Library sidebar.
  • Formatted export to Excel (.xlsx), CSV, and clean print-ready PDF reports.
Design Assurance
Proactive Risk Elimination

6. Process FMEA (PFMEA)

Manufacturing operations and packing/shipment risk assessment
PFMEA scrutinizes shop-floor manufacturing compounding, mixing, reaction kinetics, and distribution logistics to guarantee process repeatability and zero-defect delivery.
Key Features & Capabilities 5 Features
  • 2 Core Modules: 5. Process Master (Manufacturing Operations) & 6. Packing & Shipment.
  • Compounding failure mode analysis: Viscosity drift, exotherm overshoot, phase separation, particle agglomeration.
  • Packaging & logistics analysis: Seal rupture, moisture contamination, freezing during transit, container deformation.
  • Action plan tracking with revised RPN verification after shop-floor engineering controls are implemented.
  • Instant spreadsheet import/export (Excel, CSV) and executive PDF generation.
Operational Excellence
Zero-Defect Production Delivery

7. Control Plans

Shop-floor inspection criteria, testing frequencies, and reaction plans
The Control Plan operationalizes FMEA risk findings into concrete, executable shop-floor verification routines. It defines exact measurement methods, sampling rates, and containment reaction plans.
Key Features & Capabilities 6 Features
  • Process Step mapping and Key Characteristic classification (CTQ - Critical to Quality / KPC - Key Product Characteristic).
  • Specification & tolerance limits (e.g. Viscosity ±500 cPs, Solids % ≥ 65%, Peel Strength ≥ 15 N/cm).
  • Defined measurement techniques and lab test equipment (e.g. Brookfield Viscometer, Tensile Tester).
  • Sampling size, inspection frequency, and Statistical Process Control (SPC) methods.
  • Clear reaction plans instructing operators on exact containment/quarantine steps if parameters drift out-of-spec.
  • Exportable and printable official control plan documents for audit readiness.
Shop-Floor Standard
Rigorous Quality Verification

8. HOD Review Portal

Dedicated executive approval gateway with high-risk checkpoint isolation
The HOD Review Portal (located at REVIEW/index.html) is the official sign-off barrier preventing unverified adhesive formulations from entering manufacturing production.
Key Features & Capabilities 6 Features
  • Dedicated executive portal interface restricted to authorized Head of Department personnel.
  • Submission queue showing all formulations in Pending Review status with submission timestamps.
  • High-risk checkpoint filter automatically flagging any row with RPN > 100 for mandatory review.
  • Full interactive inspection of DFMEA, PFMEA, and Control Plan data prior to decision.
  • Digital Sign-off: Approve or Reject with mandatory executive audit remarks.
  • Automatic document locking: Approved sheets become read-only to preserve production release integrity.
Executive Oversight
Rigorous Compliance Release

The Operation & Activities History module maintains a tamper-evident audit trail for ISO 9001 and IATF 16949 compliance, logging every critical action taken across all formulations.

  • Keyword search and category filtering across users, modules, and action categories.
  • Detailed action tracking: Formulation Creation, Sheet Edits, Approvals, Rejections, and Imports.
  • Chronological event timeline display for rapid audit readiness during quality inspections.
  • Exportable activity log for quality archive records.
Audit Readiness

Central knowledge repository for failure modes, causes, and proven controls
The History Library captures institutional knowledge from past adhesive production runs. It allows engineers to search, reuse, and insert proven failure modes directly into active worksheets.
Key Features & Capabilities 6 Features
  • Searchable database of historical adhesive failure modes (cohesive failure, gelation, viscosity drift, bubbling).

  • "Pick & Insert" functionality: 1-click import into active DFMEA or PFMEA worksheets.
  • User capability to save new custom failure mode entries directly into the central library.
  • "Restore Industry Standards" tool to re-seed factory benchmark adhesive templates.

S/O/D Rating Tables

Severity (S) — PFMEA (Impact to Plant / Ship-to-Plant / End User)

10
High: Failure may result in an acute health/safety risk for the manufacturing or assembly worker. For the end user: affects safe operation of the vehicle, or the health of the driver, passenger, or road user/pedestrian.
9
High: Failure may result in in-plant regulatory noncompliance. For the end user: noncompliance with regulations.
8
Moderately High: 100% of production run affected may have to be scrapped; may result in in-plant regulatory noncompliance or chronic health/safety risk for the worker. Ship-to-plant: shutdown greater than a full production shift, stop shipment possible, field repair/replacement required. End user: loss of primary vehicle function necessary for normal driving.
7
Moderately High: Product may be sorted and a portion (<100%) scrapped; deviation from primary process, decreased line speed or added manpower. Ship-to-plant: shutdown from 1 hour up to a full shift. End user: degradation of primary vehicle function necessary for normal driving.
6
Moderately Low: 100% of production run may have to be reworked off-line and accepted. Ship-to-plant: shutdown up to 1 hour. End user: loss of secondary vehicle function.
5
Moderately Low: A portion of the production run may have to be reworked off-line and accepted; no line shutdown but sort required. End user: degradation of secondary vehicle function.
4
Moderately Low: 100% of production run may have to be reworked in-station before processing; triggers a significant reaction plan. End user: very objectionable appearance, sound, vibration, harshness, or haptics.
3
Low: A portion of the production run may have to be reworked in-station before processing; triggers a minor reaction plan. End user: moderately objectionable appearance, sound, vibration, harshness, or haptics.
2
Low: Slight inconvenience to process, operation, or operator; no reaction plan triggered, feedback to supplier required. End user: slightly objectionable appearance, sound, vibration, harshness, or haptics.
1
Very Low: No discernible effect.

10
Very High: Affects safe operation of the vehicle and/or other vehicles, the health of driver or passenger(s), or road users/pedestrians.
9
Noncompliance with regulations.
8
High: Loss of primary vehicle function necessary for normal driving during expected service life.
7
Degradation of primary vehicle function necessary for normal driving during expected service life.
6
Moderate: Loss of secondary vehicle function.
5
Degradation of secondary vehicle function.
4
Very objectionable appearance, sound, vibration, harshness, or haptics.
3
Low: Moderately objectionable appearance, sound, vibration, harshness, or haptics.
2
Slightly objectionable appearance, sound, vibration, harshness, or haptics.
1
Very Low: No discernible effect.

Occurrence (O) — PFMEA (Prevention Control Effectiveness)

10
Extremely High (No prevention controls): No prevention controls in place.
9-8
Very High (Behavioral): Prevention controls will have little effect in preventing the failure cause.
7-6
High (Behavioral or Technical): Prevention controls are somewhat effective in preventing the failure cause.
5-4
Moderate: Prevention controls are effective in preventing the failure cause.
3-2
Low / Very Low (Best Practices): Prevention controls are highly effective in preventing the failure cause.
1
Extremely Low (Technical): Prevention controls are extremely effective in preventing the failure cause from occurring, due to design (e.g. part geometry) or process (e.g. fixture/tooling design). Failure Mode cannot be physically produced.

Occurrence (O) — DFMEA (Design/Technology Maturity & Field Experience)

10
Extremely High: First application of new technology anywhere without operating experience and/or under uncontrolled operating conditions. No verification/validation experience. Standards and best practices do not yet exist. Prevention controls not able to predict field performance or do not exist.
9
Very High: First use of design with technical innovations or materials within the company. New application or change in duty cycle/operating conditions. No verification/validation experience. Prevention controls not targeted to identify performance to specific requirements.
8
Very High: First use of design with technical innovations or materials on a new application. No verification/validation experience. Few existing standards/best practices apply. Prevention controls not a reliable indicator of field performance.
7
High: New design based on similar technology and materials. New application or change in duty cycle/operating conditions. Standards/best practices apply to the baseline design but not the innovations. Prevention controls provide limited indication of performance.
6
High: Similar to previous designs using existing technology/materials. Similar application with changes in duty cycle/operating conditions. Previous testing or field experience. Standards/design rules exist but are insufficient to ensure the failure cause won't occur. Prevention controls provide some ability to prevent a failure cause.
5
Moderate: Detail changes to a previous design using proven technology/materials. Similar application/duty cycle. Previous testing/field experience, or new design with some related test experience. Design addresses lessons learned. Best practices re-evaluated but not yet proven. Prevention controls capable of finding deficiencies and provide some indication of performance.
4
Moderate: Almost identical design with short-term field exposure. Similar application, minor change in duty cycle/operating conditions. Previous testing or field experience. Predecessor design conforms to best practices, standards, and specifications. Prevention controls capable of finding deficiencies and indicate likely design conformance.
3
Low: Detail changes to a known design (same application, minor duty-cycle change) and testing/field experience under comparable conditions, or new design with a successfully completed test procedure. Design expected to conform to standards/best practices considering lessons learned. Prevention controls predict conformance of the production design.
2
Very Low: Almost identical mature design with long field exposure. Same application, comparable duty cycle/operating conditions. Testing/field experience under comparable conditions. Design expected to conform to standards/best practices with significant confidence margin. Prevention controls indicate confidence in design conformance.
1
Extremely Low: Failure eliminated through prevention control; failure cause is not possible by design.

Detection (D) — DFMEA (Product Design Validation)

10
Very Low: Test procedure yet to be developed; test method not defined.
9
Very Low: Test method not designed specifically to detect the failure mode or cause (Pass-Fail, Test-to-Failure, Degradation Testing).
8
Low: New test method, not yet proven (Pass-Fail, Test-to-Failure, Degradation Testing).
7
Low: Proven test method for verification/validation, but timing is later in the development cycle — test failures may cause production delays for re-design/re-tooling (Pass-Fail Testing).
6-5
Moderate: Same maturity as D=7 (timing later in development cycle) — verified via Test-to-Failure (6) or Degradation Testing (5).
4-2
High: Proven test method with timing sufficient to modify production tools before release — Pass-Fail Testing (4), Test-to-Failure (3), or Degradation Testing (2).
1
Very High: Prior testing confirmed the failure mode/cause cannot occur, or detection methods proven to always detect it.

Detection (D) — PFMEA (Process Design Validation)

10
Very Low: No testing or inspection method established or known; the failure mode will not or cannot be detected.
9
Very Low: Unlikely the testing/inspection method will detect the failure mode; not easily caught by random or sporadic audits.
8-7
Low: Test/inspection method not proven effective/reliable — human inspection (8) or machine-based inspection equipment (7) that "should" detect the failure.
6-5
Moderate: Test/inspection method proven effective/reliable — human inspection (6) or machine-based inspection (5), including product sample checks, that "will" detect the failure.
4-3
High: Automated detection system proven effective/reliable — detects downstream (4) or in-station (3), preventing outflow via a robust reject-control system.
2
High: Machine-based detection proven effective/reliable that detects the cause and prevents the failure mode (discrepant part) from being produced.
1
Very High: Failure mode cannot be physically produced as-designed/processed, or detection methods proven to always detect it.
